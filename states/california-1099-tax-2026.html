How California Taxes 1099 Freelancers & S-Corps in 2026

California is the most expensive state in America to operate an S-Corporation in 2026—meaning freelancers and LLC owners must run the real math before filing IRS Form 2553. Every California LLC and S-Corp owes the mandatory $800 annual Franchise Tax Board (FTB) minimum tax, and S-Corps additionally pay a 1.5% entity-level tax on net California income (with $800 as the floor, so $120,000 of net S-Corp profit triggers $1,800 in state entity tax). On top of that, your W-2 salary and K-1 pass-through income are still taxed under California's 1%–13.3% personal income brackets.

Real California LLC vs. S-Corp Break-Even Analysis

Despite California's 1.5% S-Corp entity tax and $2,100 in typical payroll software (Gusto) plus Form 1120-S CPA fees, the 15.3% federal self-employment tax savings still pull ahead decisively once net profit crosses roughly $95,000–$100,000. At $120,000 in net 1099 profit, switching from a Sole Proprietorship to a California S-Corp with a $54,000 reasonable W-2 salary cuts federal FICA/SE tax by nearly $8,700—leaving several thousand dollars in true net annual savings even after California's $1,800 S-Corp tax and compliance overhead. High-earning California S-Corp owners ($250k+) can also elect California's Pass-Through Entity (PTE) Elective Tax (AB 150) to bypass the federal $10,000 SALT deduction cap!

4 Legal Ways California Solopreneurs Cut 1099 Taxes in 2026

  • Wait until consistent net profit exceeds ~$95,000/yr before electing S-Corp status in California so federal SE tax savings comfortably beat the 1.5% FTB entity tax ($800 min) and Form 1120-S CPA costs.
  • Remember California does NOT conform to the federal 20% QBI (Section 199A) deduction on your state return—though you still claim the full 20% QBI deduction on your federal IRS return.
  • If your California S-Corp pays over $10,000 in state income tax, ask your CPA about the California Pass-Through Entity Elective Tax (PTET) to convert non-deductible state tax into a federal business deduction.
  • Max out a Solo 401(k) (up to $23,500 employee deferral + 25% of W-2 salary as employer match) to slash both Federal 22%–24% and California 9.3% taxes.

Frequently Asked Questions: California 1099 & S-Corp Taxes

At what profit is an S-Corp worth it in California in 2026?

Because California charges a 1.5% S-Corp entity tax ($800 minimum) plus ~$2,100/yr for payroll and Form 1120-S CPA prep, most California solopreneurs need at least $95,000–$100,000 in annual net profit for an S-Corp to clearly outperform a Sole Prop/LLC.

Does California allow the 20% QBI deduction for 1099 freelancers?

You get the 20% QBI deduction on your federal IRS return, but California does not conform to Section 199A on state returns.

Do California S-Corp owners have to pay CA SDI on their own W-2 salary?

Sole shareholders of a California S-Corp can file Form DE 459 to opt out of California State Disability Insurance (CA SDI) on their officer W-2 salary if eligible.
