How Illinois Taxes 1099 Freelancers & S-Corps in 2026

Illinois charges a flat 4.95% personal state income tax on 1099 freelance profits in 2026 with zero Chicago city income tax. However, Illinois S-Corporations and partnerships pay an additional 1.5% Personal Property Replacement Tax (PPRT) on net business income—meaning $120,000 of Illinois S-Corp profit triggers ~$1,800 in state Replacement Tax on top of your 4.95% personal return (similar in structure to California's 1.5% S-Corp tax).

Real Illinois LLC vs. S-Corp Break-Even Analysis

Because Sole Proprietorships (single-member LLCs taxed on Schedule C) are EXEMPT from Illinois's 1.5% Replacement Tax, Illinois freelancers must cross a higher break-even threshold (~$95,000 in annual net profit) before an S-Corp beats a Sole Proprietorship. Once net profit reaches $120,000+, federal 15.3% self-employment tax savings comfortably outrun both the 1.5% PPRT (~$1,800) and $2,100 in payroll/CPA overhead—still delivering $3,800+ in true net annual savings while unlocking Illinois's Pass-Through Entity (PTE) 4.95% SALT workaround.

Frequently Asked Questions: Illinois 1099 & S-Corp Taxes

Do Illinois Sole Proprietors pay the 1.5% Replacement Tax (PPRT)?

No. Single-member LLCs and Sole Proprietors filing Schedule C are exempt from Illinois's 1.5% Personal Property Replacement Tax—only S-Corps, partnerships, and trusts pay PPRT.

Is an S-Corp still worth it in Illinois despite the 1.5% Replacement Tax?

Yes, once net profit exceeds ~$95,000–$100,000/yr. At $120,000 profit, federal 15.3% SE tax savings (~$8,700) still beat the 1.5% PPRT + payroll/CPA overhead by nearly $4,000/yr.

Does Illinois allow the 20% QBI deduction on state taxes?

No. Illinois starts from Federal AGI (before the federal 20% QBI deduction), so QBI saves you federal tax only.
