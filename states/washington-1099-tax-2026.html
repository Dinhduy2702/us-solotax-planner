How Washington Taxes 1099 Freelancers & S-Corps in 2026

Washington State imposes 0% personal state income tax on 1099 freelance profits and S-Corp K-1 distributions in 2026. However, unlike Texas or Florida, Washington levies a Business & Occupation (B&O) gross receipts tax (~1.5% on service/consulting revenue above the Small Business B&O Credit threshold, plus local Seattle B&O above $100k) regardless of whether you operate as a Sole Proprietorship, LLC, or S-Corporation.

Real Washington LLC vs. S-Corp Break-Even Analysis

Because Washington's B&O tax applies identically to both Sole Proprietorships and S-Corps, switching to an S-Corp in Washington does NOT create extra state entity tax (beyond the ~$70–$200 annual Secretary of State / payroll filing cost). That keeps Washington's S-Corp break-even point low—around $70,000–$75,000 in annual net profit. On $120,000 of net consulting profit, a Seattle S-Corp owner saves over $5,400+ in net federal SE tax per year after payroll and CPA overhead, while paying 0% Washington state income tax on both W-2 salary and K-1 distributions.

Frequently Asked Questions: Washington 1099 & S-Corp Taxes

Does switching to an S-Corp avoid Washington's B&O tax?

No. Washington's B&O tax applies to gross business receipts across all entity types (Sole Prop, LLC, and S-Corp). The S-Corp benefit in Washington comes from cutting federal 15.3% SE tax and WA payroll taxes on K-1 distributions.

Do Washington 1099 freelancers owe state personal income tax?

No. Washington has 0% personal income tax on 1099 business profits, W-2 salaries, and S-Corp K-1 distributions.

Are S-Corp K-1 distributions subject to WA Cares (0.58%)?

No. WA Cares applies only to W-2 wages—making a reasonable S-Corp W-2/K-1 split even more attractive in Washington.
