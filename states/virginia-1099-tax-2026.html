How Virginia Taxes 1099 Freelancers & S-Corps in 2026

Virginia is a remarkably clean, low-friction state for 1099 contractors and S-Corporation owners in 2026—especially for Northern Virginia government, defense, and tech consultants. Virginia caps personal income tax at 5.75% (with zero local county income tax), charges NO state S-Corp minimum franchise tax (just a $50 annual LLC/SCC registration fee), and recognizes federal Form 2553 automatically.

Real Virginia LLC vs. S-Corp Break-Even Analysis

Because Virginia imposes virtually zero S-Corp entity fees (~$50–$100/yr) and no state disability payroll tax, Virginia freelancers reach S-Corp break-even right around $70,000 in annual net profit. At $120,000 in net 1099 profit, a Virginia S-Corp with a $54,000 reasonable W-2 salary nets over $5,500+ in annual after-tax savings compared to a Sole Proprietorship—and qualifies for Virginia's 5.75% Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) election to deduct Virginia state income tax on your federal return.

Frequently Asked Questions: Virginia 1099 & S-Corp Taxes

Does Virginia charge a minimum franchise tax on S-Corporations?

No. Virginia has no state minimum franchise or entity income tax on S-Corporations—only a $50 annual SCC registration fee for LLCs.

Do Virginia S-Corps need a separate state S-Corp election form?

No. Virginia automatically recognizes your federal IRS Form 2553 S-Corp election.

What is Virginia's local BPOL tax for freelancers?

Many Virginia counties and cities levy a small annual BPOL business license fee or tiny gross receipts tax (~0.2%–0.58% for professional services above local exemption thresholds like $100k in Fairfax).
