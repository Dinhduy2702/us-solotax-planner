How Texas Taxes 1099 Freelancers & S-Corps in 2026

Texas is arguably the best state in the United States for 1099 freelancers, agency owners, and S-Corporation solopreneurs in 2026. Texas levies 0% personal state income tax on Schedule C and K-1 profits, AND its 'No Tax Due' Franchise Tax threshold sits at $2.47 million in annualized revenue—meaning virtually every solo LLC and S-Corp in Austin, Dallas, and Houston pays $0 in Texas state entity tax and $0 in state income tax.

Real Texas LLC vs. S-Corp Break-Even Analysis

Because Texas adds $0 in state S-Corp franchise fees, your S-Corp break-even point drops dramatically to around $65,000–$70,000 in annual net profit (compared to $95,000+ in California or Illinois). At $120,000 in net 1099 profit, a Texas Sole Proprietor pays nearly $17,000 in federal 15.3% self-employment tax alone. Electing S-Corp status with a $54,000 reasonable W-2 salary slashes payroll tax to ~$8,262—netting over $5,500 to $6,500+ in pure annual cash savings after payroll software and Form 1120-S CPA fees, with zero state income tax drag.

4 Legal Ways Texas Solopreneurs Cut 1099 Taxes in 2026

  • Evaluate IRS Form 2553 (S-Corp election) as soon as Texas net profit crosses $65,000–$75,000/yr—zero state S-Corp fee means almost every dollar of federal SE tax savings hits your pocket.
  • Still file your annual Texas Public Information Report (PIR) by May 15 each year even though your Franchise Tax due is $0 under the $2.47M threshold.
  • Combine your Texas S-Corp with a Solo 401(k): because Texas charges 0% state tax, Roth Solo 401(k) employee deferrals lock in federal-only tax rates permanently.
  • Track every home office, software, and $0.70/mile business deduction on Schedule C / Form 1120-S to maximize both federal tax savings and the 20% QBI deduction.

Frequently Asked Questions: Texas 1099 & S-Corp Taxes

How much is the Texas S-Corp Franchise Tax in 2026?

$0 for any business with annualized total revenue under $2.47 million. You only file a simple annual Public Information Report (PIR) with the Texas Comptroller.

When should a Texas 1099 freelancer switch to an S-Corp?

Once annual net profit consistently exceeds $65,000–$75,000, federal self-employment tax savings typically outweigh ~$2,100 in annual Gusto payroll and Form 1120-S CPA costs by $2,500–$6,000+/yr.

Do Texas 1099 contractors pay quarterly state estimated taxes?

No. Texas freelancers only pay quarterly federal IRS Form 1040-ES estimated taxes (Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15).
